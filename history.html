History of Pasta

Learn how a simple mixture of flour and water became one of the most loved foods around the world.

What is Pasta?

Pasta is a traditional food usually made from an unleavened dough of wheat flour (most commonly durum wheat semolina) mixed with water or eggs. The dough is formed into different sheets, strips, tubes, or shapes and then cooked by boiling or baking. It can be made fresh at home or dried so that it lasts for months on a shelf.

Early History

People have been making noodle-like and pasta-like foods for thousands of years. Ancient civilizations in the Mediterranean region, such as the Greeks and Romans, made flat sheets of dough called lagana, which were layered with meat and baked in ovens. In Asia, noodles made from millet and wheat also existed in ancient China. However, traditional Italian pasta developed independently in the Mediterranean using hard durum wheat.

Pasta in Italy

By the 12th century, dried pasta production was well established in Sicily and southern Italy. Because dried pasta could be stored for a long time without spoiling, it became very useful for sailors and travelers. Cities like Naples had an ideal coastal climate for drying pasta in the open air. In the late 18th century, when tomatoes started being cooked into sauces in Italy, pairing pasta with tomato sauce became a daily meal for people across all social classes.

How Pasta Became Popular

During the late 19th and early 20th centuries, millions of Italians migrated to countries like the United States, Argentina, Canada, and across Europe. They brought their traditional pasta shapes and family recipes with them. Small neighborhood shops and family restaurants helped introduce spaghetti, macaroni, and ravioli to people all over the world.

Modern Pasta

Today, pasta is produced on a large scale using automated machines that mix, press, cut, and dry the dough cleanly and quickly. Supermarkets carry dozens of shapes, as well as whole wheat, gluten-free (made from rice, corn, or lentils), and fresh refrigerated pastas. Even with modern machinery, the basic ingredients of flour, water, and salt remain the same.
